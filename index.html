The problem

April is lost to a question the file already answered.

Document collection still runs on a generic organizer, a shared inbox, and a partner asking whether the 1099 showed up. The work is repetitive, and it lands when the firm is already short of preparers.

  1. 01

    The organizer does not know the client

    A retiree gets asked for a K-1. A freelancer gets asked for a mortgage statement. People stall on questions that were never theirs, and staff still have to translate what came back.

  2. 02

    Files arrive in every channel

    Email, text, a portal, a photo at the front desk. Someone retypes the status into a spreadsheet that is already a week behind.

  3. 03

    Reminders keep going after the document is in

    A blanket nudge lists the whole organizer. The client who already sent the W-2 gets the same note as the client who has sent nothing.

  4. 04

    March becomes a status desk

    "Did you get my documents?" is not a tax question. It is a tracking failure, and it consumes the weeks the firm needed for preparation and review.

Product

A checklist that shrinks itself.

DocChase sits in front of preparation. It does not file the return. It gets the firm to a complete file without another round of phone tag.

Per-client lists from last year

W-2s, 1099-NEC, INT, DIV, and B, K-1s, Form 1098, property tax, charitable receipts, childcare, and the odd item that client always has. Not a master list of every form in the catalog.

Reminders that list only the gaps

Each note names what is still open. When the last item matches, the cadence stops. No one is nudged for a document the firm already has.

One upload link per checklist

The client does not pick a folder or learn a portal. The link belongs to that checklist, and staff can see the same queue.

A match, with a year check

The prototype classifies a file name onto a checklist line and shows a confidence score. A 2025 form on a 2026 request is flagged instead of marked received.

A board the whole office can read

Requested, partially received, ready to prepare, in preparation, in review, waiting on client, filed. Drag a card or use Move.

How it works

Four steps between the organizer and the return.

01

Build the list

Start from last year's profile so the request matches the person, the trust, or the shop.

02

Send one link

The client gets a short list and a single place to put files. Staff stop collecting from four inboxes.

03

Match what arrives

A simulated classifier proposes the checklist line, scores the match, and refuses a prior-year form.

04

Work the board

Ready files move into preparation. Open files stay on a reminder that names only the gap.

FAQ

Straight answers.

Who is DocChase for?

Small accounting, bookkeeping, and tax-prep firms, roughly 1 to 30 people, where partners and staff still chase source documents themselves.

Does this prototype send email or text messages?

No. Reminders are previews. The page says so. Changing the cadence only changes what the preview and the queue would do.

Is the matching real AI?

No. The upload portal is a labeled simulation. It uses rules on the file name: form type, a payer word, and a four-digit year. It does not read the file.

Does DocChase prepare or file the return?

No. It ends where preparation starts. The pipeline tracks the handoff through review and filing, but it is not tax software.

Can a firm keep its current portal?

That is the point of the product direction: one link and a status the firm can trust. This prototype does not connect to Drake, UltraTax, QuickBooks, or a client portal.

Where does the waitlist go?

Into local storage in this browser, under a key this page controls. There is no account and no server. Resetting the demo inside the app does not clear the waitlist.
